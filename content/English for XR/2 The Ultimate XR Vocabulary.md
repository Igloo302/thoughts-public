## The Ultimate XR Vocabulary

#vocal
#### **Part 1: 核心概念 (Core Concepts) - 15 words**

1. **Immersion (沉浸感):** The feeling of being deeply present within a virtual environment.
    
    - _Example:_ To increase **immersion**, we need to ensure the spatial audio is accurate.
        
2. **Presence (临场感):** The psychological state of feeling truly "there" in the virtual world.
    
    - _Example:_ Realistic hand-tracking creates a powerful sense of **presence**.
        
3. **Mixed Reality (MR) (混合现实):** The merging of real and virtual worlds to produce new environments where physical and digital objects co-exist.
    
    - _Example:_ Our goal is to build a true **Mixed Reality** experience where virtual objects are indistinguishable（难区分的,不能分辨的） from real ones.
        
4. **Augmented Reality (AR) (增强现实):** Overlaying computer-generated images on the user's view of the real world.
    
    - _Example:_ The new **AR** feature allows users to see virtual furniture in their real living room.
        
5. **Virtual Reality (VR) (虚拟现实):** A completely computer-generated simulation of a three-dimensional environment.
    
    - _Example:_ In **VR**, users can be transported to entirely new worlds.
        
6. **Digital Twin (数字孪生):** A dynamic, high-fidelity virtual model of a real-world object, system, or process.
    
    - _Example:_ The client wants a **Digital Twin** of their factory for remote training.
        
7. **Metaverse (元宇宙):** A collective virtual shared space, created by the convergence of virtually enhanced physical reality and persistent virtual space.
    
    - _Example:_ Our platform is designed to be a foundational piece of the open **Metaverse**.
        
8. **Embodiment (具身感):** The sensation that a virtual body (avatar) is one's own body.
    
    - _Example:_ Accurate avatar IK (Inverse Kinematics) is crucial for a strong sense of **embodiment**.
        
9. **Volumetric Video (体积视频):** A technique that captures a three-dimensional space, creating a hologram-like video that can be viewed from any angle.
    
    - _Example:_ We are using **Volumetric Video** to record the CEO's keynote so users can feel like he is in the room with them.
        
10. **Use Case (用例):** A specific situation in which a product could be used.
    
    - _Example:_ We must define the primary **use case**: is this for gaming or productivity?
        
11. **Persistence (持久化):** The ability for virtual objects to remain in the same place in the world between sessions.
    
    - _Example:_ **Persistence** is key; when a user leaves a virtual note on a wall, it should be there when they return.
        
12. **Cross-Platform (跨平台):** Software that can run on different types of hardware or operating systems.
    
    - _Example:_ To maximize our user base, the new social app must be **cross-platform**.
        
13. **HMD (Head-Mounted Display) (头戴式显示器):** A display device, worn on the head, that has a small display optic in front of one or each eye.
    
    - _Example:_ The industrial design team is working on making the next-generation **HMD** lighter.
        
14. **Form Factor (产品形态/外形):** The size, shape, and physical specifications of a hardware device.
    
    - _Example:_ Achieving a glasses-like **form factor** is the biggest challenge for all-day AR.
        
15. **Degrees of Freedom (DOF) (自由度):** The number of ways an object can move. 3DOF is rotational; 6DOF adds positional movement.
    
    - _Example:_ This app requires users to walk around, so it must be designed for **6DOF** tracking.
        

#### **Part 2: 硬件 & 光学 (Hardware & Optics) - 15 words**

16. **Passthrough (影像透视):** Using cameras to show the user a real-time video feed of their surroundings.
    
    - _Example:_ The new full-color, low-latency **passthrough** enables compelling MR experiences.
        
17. **Field of View (FOV) (视场角):** The extent of the observable world seen at any given moment.
    
    - _Example:_ A wider **FOV** significantly enhances immersion, but it often increases the device's size.
        
18. **Waveguide (光波导):** A technology that guides light waves through a thin, transparent material, used in many AR glasses.
    
    - _Example:_ Our **waveguide** display offers high transparency but comes with a trade-off in brightness.
        
19. **IPD (Interpupillary Distance) (瞳距):** The distance between the centers of a user's pupils.
    
    - _Example:_ The software needs to guide the user to adjust the physical **IPD** for a clear image.
        
20. **Eye Box (眼动范围):** The area where a user can place their eye and still see the entire display clearly.
    
    - _Example:_ A larger **eye box** makes the glasses much easier and more comfortable to wear.
        
21. **Vergence-Accommodation Conflict (VAC) (辐辏调节冲突):** The mismatch between where the eyes converge and where they focus, a major cause of eye strain.
    
    - _Example:_ Placing UI elements too close to the user can worsen the **Vergence-Accommodation Conflict**.
        
22. **Brightness (亮度):** Measured in nits; the amount of light emitted from the display.
    
    - _Example:_ We need at least 1000 nits of **brightness** for the display to be usable outdoors.
        
23. **Contrast (对比度):** The ratio between the brightest white and the darkest black a display can produce.
    
    - _Example:_ The new display technology offers a much higher **contrast** ratio, making colors appear more vibrant.
        
24. **Refresh Rate (刷新率):** The number of times per second the display updates the image, measured in Hertz (Hz).
    
    - _Example:_ We are targeting a 90Hz **refresh rate** to ensure a smooth and comfortable visual experience.
        
25. **Latency (延迟):** The time delay between a user's action and the system's response.
    
    - _Example:_ The motion-to-photon **latency** must be under 20ms to prevent motion sickness.
        
26. **IMU (Inertial Measurement Unit) (惯性测量单元):** A sensor that tracks rotation and acceleration, key for head tracking.
    
    - _Example:_ The **IMU** data helps predict head motion when camera tracking is temporarily lost.
        
27. **Haptics (触觉反馈):** The use of touch sensations (e.g., vibrations) to provide feedback.
    
    - _Example:_ Let's add subtle **haptics** to confirm a successful button press.
        
28. **SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) (即时定位与地图构建):** The core technology for tracking a device's position in an unknown environment.
    
    - _Example:_ A robust **SLAM** system is the foundation for all world-anchored AR experiences.
        
29. **Inside-Out Tracking (由内向外追踪):** Tracking that uses cameras on the headset itself to map the environment.
    
    - _Example:_ **Inside-out tracking** makes the device portable, as it doesn't require external sensors.
        
30. **Outside-In Tracking (由外向内追踪):** Tracking that uses external sensors (e.g., base stations) to determine the headset's position.
    
    - _Example:_ **Outside-in tracking** is generally more precise but limits the user's play area.
        

#### **Part 3: 交互 & 用户体验 (Interaction & UX) - 25 words**

31. **Affordance (示能):** A cue on an object that suggests how it should be used.
    
    - _Example:_ A virtual button needs a clear visual **affordance**, like a shadow, to show it's pressable.
        
32. **User Intent (用户意图):** What the user is trying to accomplish.
    
    - _Example:_ The system needs to be better at predicting **user intent** from their gaze and hand gestures.
        
33. **Direct Manipulation (直接操控):** The feeling of directly "touching" and moving virtual objects.
    
    - _Example:_ **Direct manipulation** with hand-tracking is the most intuitive way to interact with 3D content.
        
34. **Indirect Manipulation (间接操控):** Interacting with distant objects via a medium, like a laser pointer.
    
    - _Example:_ For selecting items on a far wall, we need an **indirect manipulation** technique like raycasting.
        
35. **Raycasting (射线投射):** A method of sending out a virtual ray to detect which object it hits first.
    
    - _Example:_ We use **raycasting** from the controller to allow users to select UI elements from a distance.
        
36. **Gaze (注视):** The direction in which the user is looking, often used as an input.
    
    - _Example:_ The interaction is simple: **gaze** at an object to highlight it.
        
37. **Dwell (悬停):** A selection mechanism where a user holds their gaze or cursor on an item for a set duration.
    
    - _Example:_ The **dwell** time for selection is currently set to 1.5 seconds.
        
38. **Reticle (准星):** A cursor, often at the center of the view, that indicates the user's gaze point.
    
    - _Example:_ The **reticle** should change shape to provide feedback when hovering over an interactive target.
        
39. **Hand Tracking (手部追踪):** Technology that allows users to use their real hands for interaction in XR.
    
    - _Example:_ Implementing robust **hand tracking** will allow us to create a controller-free experience.
        
40. **Gesture (手势):** A specific hand movement that the system recognizes as a command.
    
    - _Example:_ The "pinch" **gesture** is universally used for selection.
        
41. **Onboarding (新手引导):** The process of introducing a new user to an application.
    
    - _Example:_ Data shows users are dropping off during our **onboarding**, so we must simplify it.
        
42. **User Comfort (用户舒适度):** Ensuring the experience doesn't cause physical or mental strain.
    
    - _Example:_ To maintain **user comfort**, we must avoid uncontrollable camera movements.
        
43. **Cybersickness (晕动症):** Nausea caused by a sensory mismatch in XR.
    
    - _Example:_ A drop in framerate is the leading cause of **cybersickness**.
        
44. **Ergonomics (人体工程学):** Designing for comfort and efficiency.
    
    - _Example:_ From an **ergonomics** perspective, requiring users to hold their arms up for a long time is poor design.
        
45. **Cognitive Load (认知负荷):** The amount of mental effort required to use an application.
    
    - _Example:_ Displaying too much information at once increases the user's **cognitive load**.
        
46. **Proprioception (本体感觉):** The sense of the relative position of one's own parts of the body.
    
    - _Example:_ The avatar's unnatural arm movement creates a conflict with the user's **proprioception**.
        
47. **Skeuomorphism (拟物化):** Designing virtual objects to resemble their real-world counterparts.
    
    - _Example:_ A **skeuomorphic** design for the virtual radio makes it instantly familiar.
        
48. **Diegetic UI (叙事化界面):** UI that exists within the 3D world of the experience.
    
    - _Example:_ Let's use a **diegetic UI**: the player's health is shown on their wrist-mounted watch.
        
49. **Volumetric UI (体积化界面):** UI that has depth and volume in 3D space.
    
    - _Example:_ A **volumetric UI** can be interacted with from multiple angles.
        
50. **Fidelity (保真度):** The level of detail and realism in a prototype or final product.
    
    - _Example:_ We will start with a low-**fidelity** prototype to test the core user flow.
        
51. **User Journey (用户旅程):** The path a user takes to accomplish a goal within the application.
    
    - _Example:_ We need to map out the entire **user journey**, from app discovery to completing the main task.
        
52. **Persona (用户画像):** A fictional character created to represent a user type.
    
    - _Example:_ Our primary **persona**, "Alex the Architect," needs tools for precise 3D model manipulation.
        
53. **Wireframe (线框图):** A low-fidelity layout of an interface, focusing on structure.
    
    - _Example:_ The designer will present the initial **wireframes** for the new menu system tomorrow.
        
54. **Prototype (原型):** An early, interactive sample of a product used for testing.
    
    - _Example:_ Let's build a quick **prototype** in Unity to test the feel of the new interaction.
        
55. **Spatial Audio (空间音频):** 3D sound that makes audio sources feel located in space.
    
    - _Example:_ With **spatial audio**, users can hear footsteps approaching from behind them.
        

#### **Part 4: 图形 & 性能 (Graphics & Performance) - 20 words**

56. **Render (渲染):** The process of generating an image from a model.
    
    - _Example:_ We need to optimize our assets to reduce the time it takes to **render** each frame.
        
57. **Real-time Rendering (实时渲染):** The process of rendering images fast enough to create the illusion of motion.
    
    - _Example:_ **Real-time rendering** at 90 FPS is the minimum requirement for a smooth VR experience.
        
58. **Framerate / FPS (帧率):** The number of images displayed per second.
    
    - _Example:_ The **framerate** is dropping below 72 FPS, which will cause judder.
        
59. **Frame Drop (掉帧):** When the system fails to render a frame in time, causing stuttering.
    
    - _Example:_ The complex physics simulation is causing significant **frame drops**.
        
60. **Jitter (抖动):** Apparent shaking of virtual objects, often caused by tracking inaccuracies or unstable framerate.
    
    - _Example:_ We are observing a lot of positional **jitter** on world-anchored objects.
        
61. **Optimization (优化):** The process of making a system more efficient.
    
    - _Example:_ **Optimization** is not an afterthought; we need to plan for it from day one.
        
62. **Occlusion (遮挡):** When a real object blocks a virtual one, or vice-versa.
    
    - _Example:_ Proper **occlusion** is essential for making virtual objects feel grounded in reality.
        
63. **Shader (着色器):** A program that defines the surface appearance of a 3D object (color, texture, lighting).
    
    - _Example:_ The engineer is writing a custom **shader** to create a realistic glass effect.
        
64. **Texture (纹理):** A 2D image applied to the surface of a 3D model to add detail.
    
    - _Example:_ The high-resolution **texture** makes the virtual brick wall look incredibly real.
        
65. **Polygon (多边形):** The basic shape (usually a triangle) that makes up the surface of a 3D model.
    
    - _Example:_ This model has too many **polygons**; we need to simplify it.
        
66. **Mesh (网格):** The collection of vertices, edges, and faces that define the shape of a 3D object.
    
    - _Example:_ The device scans the room and generates a 3D **mesh** of the environment.
        
67. **Draw Call (绘制调用):** A command from the CPU to the GPU to draw an object. Too many can be a performance bottleneck.
    
    - _Example:_ We can reduce **draw calls** by merging multiple small objects into one.
        
68. **LOD (Level of Detail) (细节层次):** An optimization technique using different versions of a model at different distances.
    
    - _Example:_ We need to create three **LOD** levels for each asset to maintain performance.
        
69. **Foveated Rendering (注视点渲染):** Rendering where the user is looking in high detail and the periphery in low detail.
    
    - _Example:_ **Foveated rendering** requires eye-tracking but can provide a massive performance boost.
        
70. **Scene Understanding (场景理解):** The ability of a device to recognize surfaces and objects in the environment.
    
    - _Example:_ Good **scene understanding** allows the virtual character to navigate the real room realistically.
        
71. **Plane Detection (平面检测):** A specific part of scene understanding that identifies flat surfaces like floors, walls, and tables.
    
    - _Example:_ **Plane detection** allows the user to place virtual objects on real surfaces.
        
72. **Budget (性能预算):** The maximum amount of a resource (e.g., polygons, CPU time) a feature is allowed to use.
    
    - _Example:_ The design team must stay within the performance **budget** for this new feature.
        
73. **Bottleneck (瓶颈):** The part of the system that limits overall performance.
    
    - _Example:_ The CPU, not the GPU, has been identified as the performance **bottleneck**.
        
74. **Asset (资产):** Any digital item that goes into the application, such as a 3D model, sound file, or texture.
    
    - _Example:_ The art team is responsible for creating all the visual **assets**.
        
75. **SDK (Software Development Kit) (软件开发工具包):** A set of tools and libraries for developing applications on a specific platform.
    
    - _Example:_ We need to download the latest Android XR **SDK** to get access to the new hand-tracking APIs.
        

#### **Part 5: 产品 & 项目管理 (Product & Project Management) - 25 words**

76. **Roadmap (路线图):** A high-level, strategic plan that outlines the evolution of a product over time.
    
    - _Example:_ This new feature request is not on our Q4 **roadmap**.
        
77. **Sprint (冲刺):** A short, time-boxed period in Agile development (usually 1-4 weeks) where a team works to complete a set amount of work.
    
    - _Example:_ We have three key features to deliver in this **sprint**.
        
78. **Backlog (待办事项列表):** A prioritized list of features, changes, and fixes for a product.
    
    - _Example:_ I've added your idea to the product **backlog** for future consideration.
        
79. **Stakeholder (利益相关者):** Anyone who is affected by a project (e.g., management, legal, marketing).
    
    - _Example:_ We need to get buy-in from all key **stakeholders** before we proceed.
        
80. **Bandwidth (带宽/余力):** A person's or team's capacity to take on more work.
    
    - _Example:_ The design team has no **bandwidth** for new requests this month.
        
81. **Blocker (阻塞项):** An issue that prevents a team from making progress.
    
    - _Example:_ We have a **blocker**: we are waiting for the API from Google's team.
        
82. **Dependency (依赖关系):** A situation where one task cannot be completed until another is finished.
    
    - _Example:_ The front-end work has a **dependency** on the completion of the back-end API.
        
83. **Proof of Concept (PoC) (概念验证):** A small project to test the feasibility of a core idea.
    
    - _Example:_ Let's build a **PoC** to see if the new tracking algorithm is viable.
        
84. **MVP (Minimum Viable Product) (最小可行产品):** A version of a product with just enough features to be usable by early customers.
    
    - _Example:_ The **MVP** will only include the core viewing functionality, not the editing tools.
        
85. **Dogfooding (内部测试):** Using your own product internally to find bugs before a public release.
    
    - _Example:_ We will start **dogfooding** the new build next Monday.
        
86. **User Story (用户故事):** A simple description of a feature from an end-user's perspective.
    
    - _Example:_ The **user story** is: "As a new user, I want a simple tutorial, so that I can learn the basic controls."
        
87. **Acceptance Criteria (验收标准):** The conditions that a feature must satisfy to be considered complete.
    
    - _Example:_ One of the **acceptance criteria** is that the app must load in under 3 seconds.
        
88. **Trade-off (权衡):** A decision that involves losing one quality in return for gains in another.
    
    - _Example:_ We have to make a **trade-off** between visual fidelity and battery life.
        
89. **Alignment (对齐):** Ensuring that all team members and stakeholders have a shared understanding and agreement.
    
    - _Example:_ Let's schedule a meeting to ensure we have **alignment** on the Q1 priorities.
        
90. **Deliverable (交付物):** A tangible or intangible product or result that must be provided upon completion of a project.
    
    - _Example:_ The main **deliverable** for this week is a functional prototype.
        
91. **Scope (范围):** The defined features and functions of a product or project.
    
    - _Example:_ Adding that feature is considered out of **scope** for the MVP.
        
92. **Scope Creep (范围蔓延):** The tendency for a project's scope to expand beyond its original objectives.
    
    - _Example:_ We need to be careful to avoid **scope creep** and stick to the planned features for this release.
        
93. **KPI (Key Performance Indicator) (关键绩效指标):** A measurable value that demonstrates how effectively a company is achieving key business objectives.
    
    - _Example:_ Our primary **KPI** for this feature is user retention.
        
94. **Metric (指标):** A standard of measurement used to track and assess the status of a specific process.
    
    - _Example:_ We will track the **metric** of "average session length" to measure engagement.
        
95. **A/B Testing (A/B测试):** A method of comparing two versions of a webpage or app against each other to determine which one performs better.
    
    - _Example:_ Let's run an **A/B test** on the button color to see which one gets more clicks.
        
96. **Qualitative Data (定性数据):** Descriptive data gathered from observations, interviews, or user feedback.
    
    - _Example:_ The **qualitative data** from user interviews suggests that the UI is confusing.
        
97. **Quantitative Data (定量数据):** Numerical data that can be measured.
    
    - _Example:_ The **quantitative data** shows that 70% of users drop off at the login screen.
        
98. **Iteration (迭代):** The repetitive process of refining a product based on feedback and testing.
    
    - _Example:_ This is the third **iteration** of the design; we've incorporated feedback from the last two user tests.
        
99. **Triage (分诊/优先级排序):** The process of sorting and prioritizing bugs or tasks.
    
    - _Example:_ We have a weekly bug **triage** meeting to decide which issues to fix first.
        
100. Postmortem (复盘): A process to analyze a completed project to identify what went well and what could be improved for the next time.
    
    * Example: The project postmortem revealed that our initial time estimates were too optimistic.
